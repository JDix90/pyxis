# The nine names — prose draft (Horizon 9 commit 5, S8)

**Status: FIRST DRAFT, awaiting approval. Nothing built.** The gate that
worked for the UNSOUNDED fragments, for Directive 9 and for the far marks:
read it aloud, veto freely. Two decisions are marked **N1–N2**.

## What page 6 already promises

Directive 9's last page ships today as one clause and one margin note:

> ATTACHED: vessels known to have filed after the effective date.
>
> *NINE NAMES. I HAVE MET TWO OF THEM. THE LAST ONE ON THE LIST IS A HULL IN
> A LIVERY NOBODY REGISTERED, LAST SEEN HEADING OUT, STILL FILING.*

Three constraints fall straight out of it, and the draft below obeys all
three:

1. **Nine names, and the player has met two.** The two met are the ones the
   game has already shown: **THE LAST RETURN**'s evacuation tender, and the
   rival's hull. Both are already in the world as places and evidence.
2. **The last name is the rival**, "a hull in a livery nobody registered,
   last seen heading out, still filing" — and the rival's wreck lies in PYX-01
   band 5, **not** in the procession. So the formation carries at most eight
   of the nine, and the ninth is a gap the player can feel.
3. **They filed after the order.** Every one of these ships did the thing
   Directive 9 forbade. That is what put them on an attachment, and the
   attachment is why they are all pointed home.

## N1 — does the rival's hull get a name?

The rival has been deliberately nameless for two horizons: pure arithmetic,
"the player only ever meets the evidence", and the annotation calls them *a
colleague* rather than anyone. Naming them now is a real loss.

**Recommended: the list names the rival's VESSEL, never the pilot.** The
eighth hull in the procession bears an empty bracket where a registry number
should be, and the ninth line of the list is a hull the Authority could not
name either — which is why it is last, and why the margin note calls it *a
livery nobody registered*. The player who has read the wreck's log knows
exactly whose hull is missing from the formation. Nobody says it.

## N2 — the register of the hull lines

Each readable hull answers a tap with its name and **one line**: what the
Authority's attachment recorded against it. These are clerical entries — the
horror is the filing cabinet, not the prose. Understandable on first read;
eerie because of what a clerk wrote down without noticing.

**Recommended as drafted below.** Every line is a fragment of a form.

## The list

The procession carries seven readable hulls. Two names on the attachment are
not in the formation: **THE LAST RETURN**'s tender (the player has already
stood at it, past the last band edge) and the ninth, unregistered.

> **1 · THE MERIDIAN CLERK**
> *Filed 41 returns after the effective date. Last transmission gave a
> position, a time, and no complaint.*

> **2 · CONSTANT USAGE**
> *A supply hull. Filed on behalf of four other crews who had no equipment
> left to file with.*

> **3 · THE PATIENT INSTRUMENT**
> *Filed the same volume nine times. The attachment notes the readings did
> not agree and does not say what was done about it.*

> **4 · SMALL HOURS**
> *Filed once, at length. The entry is longer than the vessel's entire
> service record before it.*

> **5 · THE HONEST DEPTH**
> *Refused a recall order in writing. The refusal is attached to the
> attachment.*

> **6 · WHAT THE LINE FOUND**
> *Filed a sounding with no bottom on it. Marked for review. No reviewer is
> named.*

> **7 · THE UNCOLLECTED**
> *Filed continuously for eleven years. Every return was received. None was
> ever read.*

And the two that are not here:

> **8 · [THE LAST RETURN's tender]** — the evacuation tender that never left,
> standing past the last band edge where the player has already read it.

> **9 · [no registry]** — *a hull in a livery nobody registered, last seen
> heading out, still filing.*

## What the formation says when you arrive

The mark's plate is already shipped and unchanged:

> MARK: THEY ARE ALL POINTED HOME. WE COUNTED FOUR HUNDRED AND STOPPED
> COUNTING. IF YOU READ THE HULLS, READ THEM SLOWLY.

The tap on a hull with no name — the hundreds — answers honestly:

> A HULL, POINTED HOME. NO REGISTRY, NO ATTACHMENT, NO NAME ON THIS ONE.

The refusal that keeps the formation from being salvage, on any attempt to
crack or mine:

> NOTHING HERE IS SALVAGE. THE HULLS ARE FULL AND NOBODY HAS TOUCHED THEM.

And the deposit — the layup's verb, once, the only other thing you can do:

> IT IS IN THE CACHE. SOMEBODY WILL NEED IT. THAT IS THE WHOLE ARRANGEMENT.

*(If S7's connection is taken, the deposit's response instead reads:)*

> LEFT WITH THE PROCESSION. THEY CARRY THINGS PAST THE END OF WHERE THEY WERE
> GOING. THAT IS WHAT THE NAME MEANT FIRST.

## S7, carried here — the two Processions

**THE PROCESSION** is a living nomad fleet with a roaming port. **THE
PROCESSION'S END** is a formation of dead ships all pointed home. The
recommendation approved was to connect rather than rename, and one line does
it: the nomad fleet is named **for** this place — a culture that takes its
name from where a road stops, and keeps moving anyway. Nothing mechanical
links them; the fleet never comes here, and nobody explains it. The only
place the connection is ever stated is the deposit line above.

# The filed return and the far marks — prose draft (Horizon 9)

**Status: DRAFT FOR REVIEW. No code exists yet, deliberately** — the gate that
worked for the UNSOUNDED fragments and Directive 9. Read it aloud. Veto
freely. Four decisions are marked **R1–R4**.

The register rule (§92) governs every line: understandable on first read,
eerie by what it means, never by being hard to parse. The far deep is the
furthest this game will ever be from home, so it carries the strangest
content — but a pilot always knows what they were just told.

---

## A. The verb (commit 0)

The tap on a deep foreign beacon, for a pilot who has heard the request and
carries unfiled cells:

> SOMEBODY ELSE'S BEACON — A PAGE IS CLIPPED TO THE MAST. HOLD TO READ IT,
> OR HOLD PAST IT TO FILE YOUR RETURN WITH THE NETWORK

**R1 — the two-stage hold.** My recommendation for one mast carrying two
verbs: the first hold-completion reads the page (3s, as shipped), and
*continuing to hold* rolls into the file (another 3s, the gauge relabels).
One gesture, two stops, the deeper commitment literally deeper in the hold.
The alternative is filing only from beacons whose page you have already read,
keeping one-hold-one-verb. The two-stage version reads better in the hand;
the alternative is simpler to build and to explain.

The confirmation, because this one is irreversible and costs cargo (§58 —
the gauge label IS the statement of terms):

> FILING WITH THE NETWORK — NO PAYMENT, NO REGISTRY. THE RETURN WILL BE READ.

And the completion toast:

> RETURN FILED — %d CELLS, PASSED HAND TO HAND. THE HOLD IS LIGHTER.

The refusals stay exact: no unfiled cells → *"NOTHING NEW TO FILE — THE
NETWORK ALREADY HOLDS YOUR MARKS"*; request not heard → the beacon simply
does not offer (the verb is absent, not locked — a lock advertises).

## B. The plate answers (commit 0)

Next outward band crossing after a filing, once per batch:

> **THE NETWORK** — *Your return is read. It is held, and it is true, and
> somebody knows it. There is a mark at bearing %s, %s out — it is ours, and
> now it is yours to read.*

The captain's log line (terse register):

> FILED WITH THE NETWORK — THEIR ANSWER NAMES A MARK, BEARING %03d

**R2 — the sender line.** "THE NETWORK" is the working name and I am not sure
it is the right one — they have never named themselves anywhere; every name
in this arc is what somebody else calls them. Alternatives: no sender at all
(a bare plate), or **"A KEPT CHANNEL"** (the band-5 light's own vocabulary,
my lean on second reading). Decide by ear.

## C. The rival, re-read (commit 1)

Shipped annotation on a rival-filed cell reading: *somebody was first*.
Once the pilot has ever filed with the network:

> A COLLEAGUE WAS FIRST — THE NETWORK HOLDS THEIR MARK, AND YOURS

## D. The four legends (commits 2–3)

Every mark speaks twice: the buoy's plate when resolved (what the network
sounded here), then the encounter itself. The marks are honest — the network
does not lie to its own people — and the horror, where there is horror, is
that honesty out here is frightening enough.

The unreadable tap, for a pilot who has never filed:

> A SOUNDING MARK — THE NOTATION IS NOT THE AUTHORITY'S. YOU CANNOT READ IT.

### 1. THE VEIN (generous)

> MARK: DEEP LODE. THE VEIN RUNS RICHER THAN ANYTHING ON THE CHARTED SIDE
> AND IT HAS NO BOTTOM THAT WE FOUND. BRING A TORCH AND WATCH THE WEATHER.

The field: band-6 seams at multiples of the best charted yield, standing in
storm-prone open deep. The wealth is real and the warning is real; nothing
else is there. Not every gift has teeth — one mark keeping its promise
plainly is what makes the others' warnings credible.

### 2. THE LAYUP (generous)

> MARK: LAYUP. STORES CACHED BY CREWS WHO DID NOT ALL COME BACK FOR THEM.
> TAKE WHAT YOU NEED. LEAVE WHAT YOU CAN. IT HAS ALWAYS WORKED THAT WAY.

A refusers' cache: an unrigged hull holding goods and salvage at a scale no
charted wreck approaches. One crack, one-time, remembered. **R3 — "leave what
you can":** should the layup accept a deposit — cargo left behind for the
fiction's sake, no mechanical return ever? I lean yes as a pure expressive
verb (it costs the player something and pays nothing, which by this arc's own
logic is the most meaningful thing in the game), but it is one more verb to
build and it can land in a later pass without loss.

### 3. THE STILLNESS' GROUND (the dread one)

> MARK: STANDING WARNING. IT PASSES HERE. IF YOUR DRIVES GO QUIET, THEY WILL
> COME BACK. DO NOT BURN. DO NOT TRANSMIT. WAIT.

Inside the field the drives go to zero — a becalming, not damage, not EMP.
Something crosses the far edge of the sensor picture while you drift: a
shadow on the starfield, a shape the radar refuses to tier. Then the drives
return, exactly as the mark promised. On the far side, a reading — the only
place `chart_unsounded` exists outside the archives.

The one line spoken inside, ship's voice, once:

> DRIVES QUIET. THE MARK SAID THIS HAPPENS. THE MARK SAID THEY COME BACK.

Nothing explains what passed. The mark warns *that*; nothing anywhere says
*what*. This names one of the three unknowns the survey register only ever
described by its residue — and it stays described, never shown.

### 4. THE STANDING ORDER (the fight)

> MARK: HAZARD, PERMANENT. AN AUTHORITY ASSET HOLDS THIS GROUND. IT WAS NOT
> RECALLED. IT CANNOT BE REASONED WITH, BECAUSE NOBODY EVER TOLD IT ANYTHING.
> IF YOU FIGHT IT, FIGHT IT AWAKE.

A GSA heavy asset, still in service past the terminus, executing the last
order it ever received. Page 4 of Directive 9, met in person: *the machines
were never told*, as a boss. It flies Authority teal in a place the Authority
says does not exist. First-kill bonus at apex scale, and the kill line is the
arc's thesis said plainly:

> ASSET SILENCED. IT WAS DOING ITS JOB. IT WAS ALWAYS JUST DOING ITS JOB.

**R4 — should the Standing Order be hostile to everyone, or only to the
unregistered?** The crueler reading: it hails you first, in the warm shipped
register — *"REGISTERED VESSEL. THIS VOLUME IS CLOSED. RETURN TO THE CHARTED
SIDE"* — and opens fire only when you do not comply. The machine is polite,
correct, and lethal, which is the whole GSA in one encounter. I recommend the
hail-then-fire version; a mute guard is a monster, and monsters are the one
thing this game has refused from the start.

## What is deliberately not written

- Any name for the network spoken by the network. Every name in the arc is
  what somebody else calls them, and that stays true.
- Any anatomy for the Stillness — or any hint that the other two unknowns
  have marks. Two legends' worth of space is deliberately left dark for a
  later horizon: twelve marks, four kinds, and the table has room.
- Any line where the Authority notices. The Standing Order does not report
  the fight. Nobody comes. The Commission field does not move.
